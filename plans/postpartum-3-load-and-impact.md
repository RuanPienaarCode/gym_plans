PHASE 3 OF 4 — LOAD AND IMPACT PREP. This is the phase that decides whether
running goes well. Around 12 weeks post birth at the earliest — the commonly
used guidance is that returning to running before about 12 weeks is
associated with a higher rate of pelvic floor problems.

THE RETURN-TO-RUNNING CHECKS. Work these until you can do ALL of them with no
leaking, no heaviness or dragging, and no pain:

- Walk 30 minutes briskly
- Balance on one leg, 10 seconds each side
- Single-leg calf raise, 10 each side
- Single-leg glute bridge, 10 each side
- Single-leg sit-to-stand, 10 each side
- Jog on the spot, 1 minute
- Hop in place, 10 each side
- Forward bounds, 10
- Single-leg hop, 10 each side

If any of them produces a symptom, that is the thing to work on — not a
reason to give up, and not a reason to run anyway.

## Strength (mon)

- Glute Bridge | 3 x 12
- Single-leg Glute Bridge | 3 x 8/side
- Bodyweight Squat | 3 x 12
- Single-leg Sit-to-Stand | 3 x 8/side
- Dead Bug | 3 x 8/side

## Strength + Impact prep (wed)

- Single-leg Calf Raise | 3 x 10/side
- Bulgarian Split Squat | 3 x 8/leg
- Standing March | 3 x 12/side
- Side Plank | 3 x 20-30s
- Inverted Rows | 3 x 10

## Impact testing (fri)

Only the amount that stays symptom-free. Stop the moment anything changes.

- Standing March | 2 x 20/side
- Single-leg Calf Raise | 3 x 10/side
- Jog on the Spot | 1 x 30-60s
- Bird Dog | 2 x 10/side

## Walk (any)

- Recovery Walk | 1 x 30-45 min brisk

MOVE TO PHASE 4 WHEN: every check above is clean. Not most of them.
