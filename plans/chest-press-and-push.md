A chest session for a home rack that has no bench press in it. Everything here
presses from the floor, the bar or the box — which turns out to be an
advantage rather than a compromise, because the floor stops the arm before the
shoulder reaches the range where an old shoulder complains.

A bar, a pair of dumbbells and a box give four honest chest angles: flat
(Dumbbell Floor Press), decline through the dip (Straight-bar Dips),
incline-ish through elevated feet (Feet-elevated Push-ups), and the wide arc
(Dumbbell Floor Fly). That is enough. The chest is one muscle with one job —
bringing the arm across and in front of you — and it does not need eight
machines to be trained well.

DIPS ARE THE ONE TO WATCH. They put the shoulder in its most compromised
position at the bottom, which is unforgiving of any shoulder history. Stop the
descent where the upper arm reaches parallel, not lower, and if there is any
tingling down the arm, that set is over — not the rep, the set.

Ten to twenty hard sets a week is the range the evidence supports for growth.
One of these sessions plus the pressing already in a normal training week gets
you into it; two of them in a week is the top of it, not a bonus.

## Chest · Full Session (any)

Press first while you are fresh, flyes last when you are not. Rest ~90s on the
first three, ~60s after.

- Dumbbell Floor Press | 4 x 8-12
- Feet-elevated Push-ups | 3 x 8-12
- Straight-bar Dips | 3 x 6-10
- Dumbbell Floor Fly | 3 x 12-15
- Close-grip Push-ups | 3 x 10-15
- Push-ups | 1 x submax

## Chest · 12-minute Finisher (any)

Bodyweight, no setup. Tack it onto the end of a push day, or use it on a day
you have twelve minutes and a floor.

- Push-ups | 4 x 12-15
- Push-up and Rotation | 3 x 10
- Dumbbell Floor Fly | 3 x 12-15
