TWELVE WEEKS TO A HALF MARATHON. Four runs a week now, because 21.1km is the
distance where three stops being enough: easy, intervals, an easy-or-tempo
midweek run, and the long one.

THE LADDER, in kilometres: 8, 10, 12, 9, 13, 15, 16, 12, 17, 18, 14, 21. The
longest training run is 18 — you do NOT run the full distance in training,
and the last three weeks come down rather than up. Race day borrows the
fitness you built by week 10 and gives you back the freshness you spent the
last fortnight collecting. Runners who add one more long run in week 11 have
been talked out of it by better coaches than this file.

TIME ON FEET BEATS PACE on the long run. Run it a minute a kilometre slower
than you think you should. The point of a three-hour training week is the
hours, not the speed, and every injury in this plan lives in the gap between
those two ideas.

PRACTISE THE RACE, NOT JUST THE DISTANCE. Wear the shoes and the shorts you
will race in for the long runs from week 8. Eat what you plan to eat. A half
marathon is long enough to discover a chafing problem or a gel your stomach
refuses, and the week 10 long run is a far better place to find out.

## Easy Run (mon)

The shortest run of the week and the one people skip. Keep it.

- Easy Run | 1 x 30 min easy

## Intervals (wed)

Full warm-up. Stop the session — not the rep, the session — if anything
sharp shows up.

- Interval Repeats | 6 x 3 min hard, 90s jog
- Strides | 4 x 20s

## Tempo (thu)

From week 5 make this a tempo; before that keep it easy while the mileage
settles.

- Tempo Run | 1 x 5-8 km at tempo

## Long Run (sat)

Per the ladder, slow, fed and watered. This is the run the plan is built
around.

- Easy Run | 1 x per the ladder

## Recovery (sun)

- Recovery Walk | 1 x 40-50 min easy
