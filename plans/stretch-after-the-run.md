FOUR STRETCHING SESSIONS, none of them a workout. The first is the one to do
in the ten minutes after a run, while everything is still warm. The other
three are targeted — legs, shoulders, back — for the day something in
particular is tight.

NONE OF THESE HAVE A WEEKDAY. Start whichever one you need from the Plans
page. They sit alongside Rest & Recovery rather than replacing it: that plan
fills your empty days automatically and asks nothing of you; these you choose
because something specific needs attention.

WHAT STRETCHING ACTUALLY DOES, and what it does not. Held stretches reliably
increase range of motion over weeks — the gains come from accumulated weekly
time on a muscle, roughly 30 to 60 seconds a hold, repeated, several times a
week. What the evidence does NOT support is the old promise that stretching
after a run prevents injury or meaningfully reduces next-day soreness. Do it
because tight hips and calves make running feel worse and because the range
is worth having, not because you have been told it is insurance.

AFTER A RUN, NOT BEFORE ONE. A long hold leaves the muscle temporarily
weaker and less springy — irrelevant on the couch an hour later, unhelpful
five minutes before intervals. The warm-up before a run wants movement:
World's Greatest Stretch, Inch Worms, a few Strides.

Breathe out into each hold and stop at mild tension. Nothing here should be
sharp, and nothing here should leave you sore tomorrow.

## After the Run (any)

Ten minutes, in the kit you ran in, before you sit down. Calves and hips
first — they are what running actually shortens.

- Calf Stretch | 1 x 30s each position
- Hamstring Stretch | 1 x 45s/side
- IT Band and Glute Stretch | 1 x 45s/side
- Piriformis Stretch | 1 x 45s/side
- Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch | 1 x 45s/side
- Child's Pose | 1 x 60s

## Legs & Hips (any)

The longer version, for a rest day. If an ITB has ever been a problem, the
glute and piriformis holds are the two to give extra time — the band itself
does not stretch, the muscles pulling on it do.

- World's Greatest Stretch | 1 x 5/side
- Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch | 1 x 60s/side
- Hamstring Stretch | 1 x 60s/side
- IT Band and Glute Stretch | 1 x 60s/side
- Piriformis Stretch | 1 x 60s/side
- Calf Stretch | 1 x 45s each position
- Spinal Twist | 1 x 45s/side

## Shoulders & Arms (any)

For the day after pressing, or the day a desk has had you folded over a
keyboard. The band work is not a stretch — it is the small pulling volume
that keeps the joint happy, and it belongs here.

- Shoulder Circles | 1 x 10/direction
- Band Pull-Aparts | 2 x 15-20
- Band Face Pulls | 2 x 15-20
- Kettlebell Halo | 1 x 8/direction
- Dead Hang | 2 x 30s
- Child's Pose | 1 x 60s

## Back & Spine (any)

Movement first, holds second, breathing last. For a lower back that has
tightened up rather than one that hurts — sharp or radiating back pain is a
physiotherapist's problem, not a stretching plan's.

- Cat-Cow | 1 x 10 slow
- World's Greatest Stretch | 1 x 5/side
- Spinal Twist | 1 x 60s/side
- Piriformis Stretch | 1 x 45s/side
- Dead Hang | 1 x 30-45s
- Child's Pose | 1 x 60s
- Diaphragmatic Breathing | 1 x 8 slow breaths
