POSTNATAL KETTLEBELL STRENGTH. Difficulty 2 of 3. Full body, with one
kettlebell and your bodyweight. The halo warms the shoulders, the two-hand
swing comes before the hand-to-hand swing, and the floor work and the arm
bar close it out. If you have never done kettlebell work, learn the swing and
the clean from a coach or a good video before you load them.

BEFORE YOU START: check with your healthcare provider or a pelvic health
physiotherapist that postnatal exercise is safe for you. Breathe out on the
effort with a gentle pelvic floor lift. Stop any exercise that brings on
leaking, heaviness or dragging, pain, or doming up the middle of the belly,
and get it assessed rather than working around it. This plan is general
training guidance, not medical advice.

HOW TO RUN IT. Start it as a timed session and set the clock for your level:
10 minutes for beginners, 15 for intermediate, 20 for advanced. Each station
gets the default 45 seconds plus a short changeover, and the list repeats
until the time runs out. The rep numbers set the pace; you don't have to hit
them. The order is built so that the first ten stations make a complete
session on their own (swing, squat, row, lunge, clean and press, single-leg hinge), so a short clock never leaves out a whole
body part. Prefer counting reps? Work down the list once per round instead,
resting as long as you need between rounds.
