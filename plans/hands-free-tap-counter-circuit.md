FIVE EXERCISES THE APP CAN COUNT FOR YOU. Every line below is here because
the phone can do the counting while your hands are busy. No touching the
screen with your fingers between sets, no losing count at rep fourteen.

TWO WAYS TO COUNT, AND THE CHOICE IS THE WHOLE POINT OF THIS PLAN:

- TAP. The whole screen is one target. Every tap is a rep, spoken out loud as
  it lands, and taps closer together than 0.7 seconds are ignored — that
  window exists because a face on the way down to the floor hits nose, then
  chin, then forehead, and that has to stay ONE rep.
- MOTION. Press the movement button (the pulse icon) and the phone counts
  from its own accelerometer instead: it learns what your rep looks like and
  counts one every time you go down and come back up. Taps keep working while
  it runs, so a rep it misses is added by hand rather than lost.

WHICH ONE, AND WHY. Motion counting only works if the phone MOVES WITH YOU —
in a pocket, in a waistband, held at the chest. A phone lying on the floor
under a push-up is not moving, so push-ups stay a tap exercise. Everything
where the phone travels with your body can use motion.

TWO THINGS TO KNOW ABOUT MOTION MODE. On an iPhone the first press asks for
permission to use Motion & Orientation — that prompt only appears when you
press the button yourself, and declining it drops you back to tapping.
And a rep is counted on the way UP, so if you stop at the bottom of the last
one it does not land until you come back up.

WHERE TO PUT THE PHONE, EXERCISE BY EXERCISE:

- PUSH-UPS — TAP. Screen up on the floor under your chest, touched with your
  nose at the bottom of every rep. This is the one the counter was built for,
  and it doubles as a depth gate: no nose, no rep. Motion mode cannot help
  here, because the phone is on the floor and the floor is not moving.
- SIT-UPS — MOTION. Phone in a chest pocket, or held against your chest with
  both hands. Every sit-up is one clear down-and-up, which is exactly the
  shape the detector looks for. No pocket? Put it on the floor past your feet
  and tap it with your forehead instead.
- PLANK — TIMER. Start the session in TIMED CIRCUIT mode and the 60s below
  becomes a 60-second countdown that runs itself. In Reps mode you get a hold
  timer: tap once to start, tap again to stop, and it calls out every 15
  seconds while you hold. Nothing to count either way.
- PULL-UPS — MOTION. Phone in a front pocket or a waistband. The whole body
  travels a long way on a pull-up, so this is the exercise motion mode reads
  most confidently — and the one where an arrangement of props under a bar
  was always a bad idea.
- SQUATS — MOTION. Phone in a pocket. If you want the phone policing your
  DEPTH rather than just counting, put it screen up on a chair behind you at
  your target depth and tap it with your fingertips instead — motion counts
  the rep you did, a tap target counts only the rep you did properly.

WHAT THE APP STILL DOES NOT DO: watch you. There is no camera and nothing
judging your form. Motion counting reads one number — how hard the phone is
accelerating — so it counts movements, not good movements. If it ever
disagrees with you, you are right and it is wrong: tap the difference in, or
switch it off and count by hand.

ONE ROUND IS BELOW. Go through it two or three times with a minute between
rounds, or use it as a single circuit when time is short. The rep targets are
a starting point; the counter does not care what you planned.

## Counter Circuit (any)

Decide before you start whether the phone is in your pocket or on the floor
for this round, and keep it there — fumbling with a phone mid-circuit is how
the round falls apart. Pocket for sit-ups, pull-ups and squats; floor for the
push-ups.

- Push-ups | 1 x submax
- Sit-Up | 1 x 15-20
- Plank | 1 x 60s
- Pull-ups | 1 x submax
- Bodyweight Squat | 1 x 20
