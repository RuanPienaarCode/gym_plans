A FULL-BODY CIRCUIT FOR A LUNCH-HOUR GYM SESSION. Thirty minutes on the gym
floor, with the shower, the change and the drive back to work taking the other
thirty of your hour. Two or three sessions a week to build a base of plain
fitness: a pull-up bar, a dip station and a patch of floor are all it needs.

THE SHAPE OF THE THIRTY MINUTES:

- 0–5   Easy run. Conversational, with the last 30 seconds a touch quicker.
        This is the warm-up, not the workout.
- 5–27  The circuit: FOUR laps of the seven exercises below, about 45 seconds
        a station, moving straight from one to the next.
- 27–30 Stretch chest and hips, then go. This is also your overrun buffer. If
        the laps ran long, spend it on the last lap and stretch in the shower.

THE SEVEN LINES BELOW ARE ONE LAP. Written out four times they would be
twenty-eight lines of the same thing. Run once at the start, then go through
the seven lines four times with only as much rest between laps as you need
(30–60 seconds, never more). The rest is between laps, not inside them. If you
are behind the clock, do three laps with clean reps rather than four rushed.

WHY THIS ORDER:

- Jump squats go first, while your legs are warm from the run and fresh enough
  to be crisp. A tired landing is the one way to get hurt here.
- Pull-ups come next, while grip and lats are fresh.
- Push, pull and legs alternate, so no muscle is worked twice in a row: dips,
  then lunges, then push-ups, then bridges. Dips and push-ups are both
  pressing, and the lunges sit between them on purpose.
- The plank is last, so the core is not tired when the other six need it to
  hold you steady.

STARTING NUMBERS ARE DELIBERATELY LOW. The starting reps are chosen so that all
four laps stay clean. Four laps of everything is a lot of shoulder and elbow
work, especially the dips, and the pull-ups in particular are set well under
your best. If you can do six in a row, three per lap means you finish every
set with plenty in the tank.

HOW IT PROGRESSES. When all four laps feel easy and the last lap looks like
the first, add a rep. Add it to ONE exercise at a time, not all seven at once.
Rough targets for week six, if the plan is going well:

- Jump Squat 8 → 12
- Pull-ups 3 → 5
- Straight-bar Dips 5 → 8
- Forward Lunges 6 → 10 a leg
- Push-ups 8 → 12
- Glute Bridge 12 → 15, then move to the single-leg version
- Plank 30s → 45s

BURPEES. Burpees are a fair swap for the jump squats, and they are a harder
conditioning hit. They are also a push-up with a jump on the end, in a session
that already has dips, push-ups and pull-ups in it. Stay on jump squats for
the first four to six weeks, then try burpees in place of them on one session
a week and see how the shoulders feel the next morning.

WHEN A JOINT COMPLAINS. If an elbow or shoulder gets cranky, cut the dips to
five or move them to a bench (Tricep Dips) before changing anything else.
Skip the jump squats entirely on any day the knees are sore.

SCHEDULE. Monday, Wednesday, Friday is the intent, with a rest day between.
Two sessions a week is a perfectly good version of this plan: just skip
Friday. The Rest & Recovery plan will fill the other days.

## Lunch Circuit (mon)

Run once, then four laps of the seven below.

- Easy Run | 1 x 5 min easy
- Jump Squat | 1 x 8
- Pull-ups | 1 x 3
- Straight-bar Dips | 1 x 5
- Forward Lunges | 1 x 6/leg
- Push-ups | 1 x 8
- Glute Bridge | 1 x 12
- Plank | 1 x 30s

## Lunch Circuit (wed)

Same session, same order. The aim is one more rep than Monday somewhere.

- Easy Run | 1 x 5 min easy
- Jump Squat | 1 x 8
- Pull-ups | 1 x 3
- Straight-bar Dips | 1 x 5
- Forward Lunges | 1 x 6/leg
- Push-ups | 1 x 8
- Glute Bridge | 1 x 12
- Plank | 1 x 30s

## Lunch Circuit (fri)

Skip this one if you are only training twice this week.

- Easy Run | 1 x 5 min easy
- Jump Squat | 1 x 8
- Pull-ups | 1 x 3
- Straight-bar Dips | 1 x 5
- Forward Lunges | 1 x 6/leg
- Push-ups | 1 x 8
- Glute Bridge | 1 x 12
- Plank | 1 x 30s
