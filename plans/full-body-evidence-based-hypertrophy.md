THE SIZE PLAN. Three full-body sessions a week, built to the numbers the
hypertrophy research actually supports rather than to a magazine split. If
the goal for a block is muscle rather than a skill, this is the one.

THE FOUR RULES IT IS BUILT ON:

- VOLUME drives growth. Somewhere around 10 hard sets per muscle per week is
  where growth becomes reliable, and more keeps helping for a while, with the
  returns flattening out beyond roughly 20. Below is the low end of that
  range on purpose, on the assumption that something else — running, a sport —
  is also asking for recovery.
- FREQUENCY is how you fit the volume in. Splitting a muscle's weekly sets
  across two or three sessions makes each session shorter and each set
  better. Full body three times a week hits every muscle three times.
- EFFORT is what makes a set count. Stop 1–3 reps short of failure. Sets
  taken far short of that do little; sets taken TO failure cost more fatigue
  than they return, especially on the compounds and especially as you age.
- LOAD is flexible. Anything from about 5 to 30 reps builds size when the set
  is taken close enough to failure — which is exactly why a home gym with
  moderate dumbbells is not a handicap.

HOW TO PROGRESS: double progression. Pick the bottom of the rep range, add
reps week to week until you hit the top of the range on every set, then add
weight and drop back to the bottom. Log every set — the log is the programme;
without it you are guessing whether last week was heavier.

THREE DIFFERENT DAYS, not one repeated. Each session covers a squat, a hinge
or a pull, a press and an arm or core piece, but the emphasis rotates so the
same joints are not loaded identically three times a week. Sessions run about
35 minutes. Rest 2 minutes on the first two lifts of each day and 60–90
seconds on the rest — cutting rest short costs reps, and reps are the point.

WHERE IT FITS: in a block where the running is flat rather than climbing, and
not on top of a return from illness. Muscle is built by eating and sleeping as
much as by lifting; running hard on top of this and under-eating will produce
fatigue rather than size.

## Day A · Squat & Press (mon)

Quads and horizontal pressing lead. The row is here so the day is not all
pushing.

- Goblet Squat | 3 x 8-12
- Dumbbell Floor Press | 3 x 8-12
- One-arm Dumbbell Row | 3 x 8-12/side
- Standing Dumbbell Press | 2 x 10-12
- Dumbbell Curl | 2 x 10-12
- Plank | 3 x 45s

## Day B · Hinge & Pull (wed)

The heaviest day for the posterior chain, and the day the vertical pull gets
its full attention. Keep the RDL moderate and stop the set when the back
rounds.

- Romanian Deadlift | 3 x 8-10
- Pull-ups | 3 x submax
- Feet-elevated Push-ups | 3 x 10-15
- Bulgarian Split Squat | 3 x 8/leg
- Lateral Raise | 3 x 12-15
- Hanging Knee Raise | 3 x 8-12

## Day C · Single-leg & Power (fri)

Unilateral legs, the dip as the third pressing angle of the week, and swings
to finish. Lighter on the spine than Wednesday by design.

- Reverse Lunge | 3 x 10/leg
- Straight-bar Dips | 3 x 6-10
- Inverted Rows | 3 x 10-12
- Kettlebell Swing | 3 x 15
- Overhead Triceps Extension | 2 x 10-12
- Side Plank | 3 x 30-45s
