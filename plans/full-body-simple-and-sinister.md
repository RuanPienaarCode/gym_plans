SIMPLE & SINISTER — Pavel Tsatsouline's minimalist kettlebell programme, and
the most widely copied kettlebell routine there is. Two exercises. One bell.
Roughly twenty minutes. It is on this list precisely because it is the plan
you can still do in the weeks everything else falls over.

THE WHOLE PROGRAMME: 100 one-arm swings, done as 10 sets of 10 alternating
hands, and 10 Turkish get-ups, done as single reps alternating sides. That is
it. The original is written as a near-daily PRACTICE rather than a workout —
you finish feeling better than you started, never wrecked. It is programmed
here four days a week, which is the honest version for someone who is also
running twice a week. Three is fine too. Grinding it seven days
a week is not the goal and never was.

PROGRESSION IS TIME, NOT REPS. The rep count never changes — what shrinks is
the rest between sets. Start with generous rest, then compress it week by
week. The book's own benchmark for men is 100 swings inside five minutes and
10 get-ups inside ten minutes with a 32kg bell; treat that as a horizon, not
a target for this quarter.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS. Ballistic kettlebell work is a genuinely good way to
train the hinge, the posterior chain and aerobic capacity in very little
time. What it is NOT is a complete hypertrophy programme — there is no
vertical pull and no real pressing volume in it, so a quarter spent here will
build conditioning and a hinge, not a bigger chest or back. Use it as a
bridge, a travel plan, or a deload block, not as the year.

START LIGHT, AND THE REASON IS YOUR BACK. The swing is a hip snap, not a
squat and not a lift with the arms — the bell floats because the hips threw
it. If the back is doing the work you will feel it immediately, and the fix
is a lighter bell and the Kettlebell Deadlift until the hinge is automatic.
Ten sets of ten with sloppy form is a hundred chances to hurt yourself.

## Practice (mon)

Halos and deadlifts to warm the hinge, then swings, then get-ups. Rest as
long as you need between sets, especially early on.

- Kettlebell Halo | 1 x 5/direction
- Kettlebell Deadlift | 1 x 10
- Kettlebell Swing | 10 x 10
- Turkish Get-Up | 10 x 1 alternating sides

## Practice (tue)

Same session. On a day the legs feel heavy from the Tuesday run, cut the
swings to five sets and keep all ten get-ups — the get-up is the half that
does not care how tired your legs are.

- Kettlebell Halo | 1 x 5/direction
- Kettlebell Deadlift | 1 x 10
- Kettlebell Swing | 10 x 10
- Turkish Get-Up | 10 x 1 alternating sides

## Practice (thu)

Same again. Watch the clock this time — the rest between swing sets is the
only number that should be moving month to month.

- Kettlebell Halo | 1 x 5/direction
- Kettlebell Deadlift | 1 x 10
- Kettlebell Swing | 10 x 10
- Turkish Get-Up | 10 x 1 alternating sides

## Practice (fri)

Last of the week, and the one to keep easy — Saturday is the long trail run.
Finish feeling like you could do it again.

- Kettlebell Halo | 1 x 5/direction
- Kettlebell Deadlift | 1 x 10
- Kettlebell Swing | 10 x 10
- Turkish Get-Up | 10 x 1 alternating sides
