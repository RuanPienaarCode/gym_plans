PHASE 1 OF 4 — FOUNDATION. Breathing, pelvic floor connection, gentle core
and walking. Roughly the first 6 weeks after birth, but the phase matters
more than the date: start here regardless of how long ago the birth was if
you have not done this work yet.

BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE: see a pelvic health physiotherapist. A routine six-week
check is usually not a pelvic floor assessment. An individual assessment is
the single most useful thing you can do, and it is the only way to know
whether what you feel is normal healing or something that needs treating —
especially after a caesarean, a tear, an assisted delivery, or if anything
below is already happening.

STOP AND GET ASSESSED IF YOU GET: leaking urine, a feeling of heaviness,
dragging or bulging in the vagina, pain anywhere in the pelvis or back,
a bulge or doming up the midline of your belly when you lift, or bleeding
that had stopped starting again. None of these mean "push through".

This plan is general training guidance, not medical advice, and it does not
know your individual history.

## Connect (mon)

Five to ten minutes. Little and often beats one long session.

- Diaphragmatic Breathing | 1 x 10 slow breaths
- Pelvic Floor Activation | 1 x 10 long holds + 10 quick flicks
- Transverse Abdominis Activation | 1 x 5 breaths

## Connect + Move (wed)

- Diaphragmatic Breathing | 1 x 10 slow breaths
- Pelvic Floor Activation | 1 x 10 long holds + 10 quick flicks
- Glute Bridge | 2 x 8-10
- Side-lying Clam | 2 x 10/side

## Connect + Move (fri)

- Pelvic Floor Activation | 1 x 10 long holds + 10 quick flicks
- Heel Slides | 2 x 6/side
- Glute Bridge | 2 x 8-10
- Bird Dog | 2 x 6/side

## Walk (any)

Build gently: start at whatever feels easy — even 5-10 minutes — and add a
few minutes a week. Pushing a pram counts. If you feel heaviness by the end,
that walk was too long; shorten it next time rather than pushing on.

- Recovery Walk | 1 x 10-30 min easy

MOVE TO PHASE 2 WHEN: the connection work feels automatic, walking 20-30
minutes leaves no heaviness or leaking, and you are at least ~6 weeks post
birth with the go-ahead from your physio or doctor.
