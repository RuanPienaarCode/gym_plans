PHASE 2 OF 4 — REBUILD. Real strength work at low load, still with the
breathing and pelvic floor woven through every session. Roughly weeks 6-12,
but symptoms set the pace, not the calendar.

The rule for this whole phase: every lift happens on the OUT-breath with a
gentle pelvic floor lift. If you find yourself holding your breath, the load
is too heavy or the set is too long.

Same red flags as phase 1 — leaking, heaviness or dragging, pain, or doming
up the midline means stop that exercise and get it assessed rather than
working around it.

## Lower + Core (mon)

- Pelvic Floor Activation | 1 x 10 long holds
- Glute Bridge | 3 x 10-12
- Bodyweight Squat | 3 x 8-10 to a chair
- Side-lying Clam | 2 x 12/side
- Dead Bug | 2 x 6/side slow

## Upper + Core (wed)

- Pelvic Floor Activation | 1 x 10 long holds
- Inverted Rows | 3 x 8-10 (high bar, easy angle)
- Push-ups | 3 x 6-10 hands elevated
- Bird Dog | 3 x 8/side
- Side Plank | 2 x 15-20s from the knees

## Lower + Balance (fri)

- Glute Bridge | 3 x 12
- Bulgarian Split Squat | 2 x 6/leg bodyweight, shallow
- Standing March | 3 x 10/side
- Single-leg Calf Raise | 2 x 8/side
- Transverse Abdominis Activation | 1 x 5 breaths

## Walk (any)

- Recovery Walk | 1 x 30-45 min easy

MOVE TO PHASE 3 WHEN: you can walk 30 minutes briskly with no symptoms, the
strength work feels controlled rather than a struggle, and you are around
12 weeks post birth.
