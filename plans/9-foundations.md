Nine exercises, back to back, then straight to the top and again. What is
written below is ONE ROUND — it is not the whole workout. In the app this came
from you tell it how long you have and it loops the circuit on a timer until
that time is up. Twelve minutes is two or three rounds. Half an hour is five
or six.

So do not read the numbers as a target for the day. Read them as a lap, and
decide up front how many laps you are doing — that decision is much easier to
make honestly before you start than after round two.

Almost all of it is you and the floor. You need a bench, box or solid chair
for the dips, and something to row from — a low bar, a pair of rings, or the
edge of a sturdy table; everything else needs nothing at all. That is the
point of the name.

PUSH AND PULL ARE BALANCED ON PURPOSE. Push-ups and dips are both pressing, so
the inverted row sits between them. A circuit of three presses and no pull,
done several times a week, is how shoulders slowly round forward. The core
work resists movement — planks and the dead bug — rather than twisting under
load, which is the version a lower back tolerates round after round.

HOW IT PROGRESSES. Keep the same number of rounds for a fortnight. When the
last round looks like the first, add ONE thing: a round, a few reps to one
exercise, or 15 seconds to the holds — never all three at once. Log how many
rounds you did; that is the number that should climb.

It has no weekday. It sits under `(any)`, so it fills whatever day is
otherwise empty.

## The Circuit (any)

Rest between exercises only as long as it takes to get into position. The rest
is between rounds, not inside them.

- Push-ups | 1 x 15
- Bodyweight Squat | 1 x 25
- Inverted Rows | 1 x 10
- Tricep Dips | 1 x 15
- Forward Lunges | 1 x 30
- Plank | 1 x 45s
- Dead Bug | 1 x 8/side
- Up Down Squat | 1 x 30s
- Side Plank | 1 x 30s/side
