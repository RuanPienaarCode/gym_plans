SIXTEEN WEEKS TO A MARATHON, and the honest warning first: this is the only
plan in this library that can hurt you by being followed too well. The
marathon rewards patience over months and punishes enthusiasm within weeks.
Come to week 1 already able to run 12km comfortably and to hold four runs a
week. If you cannot, run the half-marathon plan first — that is not a
consolation prize, it is the correct order.

THE LADDER, in kilometres: 12, 15, 18, 14, 21, 24, 18, 26, 29, 22, 32, 26,
21, 16, 12, 42. Read the shape rather than the numbers. It climbs in threes
and steps down every third or fourth week; the longest run is 32km in week
11; and the last three weeks fall off a cliff on purpose. THE TAPER IS
TRAINING. Two easy weeks before a marathon feel like losing fitness and are
in fact where it finally arrives.

THIRTY-TWO, NOT FORTY-TWO. You never run the full distance in training. The
last 10km on race day are paid for by the twelve long runs before it and by
the taper, not by having rehearsed them once while tired.

FUELLING IS A TRAINED SKILL, and the long run is where you train it. From
week 5, eat something every 40 minutes on every run over 90 minutes, and use
exactly what you plan to use on the day. A stomach that has never been asked
to digest anything while running will refuse at 30km, and no amount of
fitness argues with that.

WHEN TO CUT A RUN SHORT: a niggle that changes how you run, any sharp joint
pain, or a resting heart rate several beats above normal for more than two
days. A missed long run costs one week. Running through what should have been
a missed long run costs the marathon.

## Easy Run (mon)

Genuinely easy. In a plan this big, the easy runs are what make the hard ones
possible, and they are the first thing people quietly speed up.

- Easy Run | 1 x 40 min easy

## Intervals or Hills (wed)

Alternate weeks: intervals one week, hills the next. From week 12 drop this
to easy running — the taper starts with the hard sessions, not the long ones.

- Interval Repeats | 6 x 4 min hard, 2 min jog
- Hill Repeats | 1 x 6-8 x 90s uphill

## Tempo (thu)

Marathon-pace work rather than all-out. This is the pace you are trying to
teach your legs to hold for four hours.

- Tempo Run | 1 x 8-12 km at tempo

## Long Run (sat)

The ladder run, slow, with food and water rehearsed exactly as on race day.

- Long Trail Run | 1 x per the ladder

## Recovery (sun)

Walk it, and do the stretching plan afterwards. Sixteen weeks is long enough
for small tightnesses to become injuries if nothing ever addresses them.

- Recovery Walk | 1 x 45-60 min easy
